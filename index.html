The Museum
of Emergence

Eight rooms. Eight rules. Not one of them knows what it is building.

Enter

A bird has no idea what a flock is.

It tracks perhaps seven neighbours. It keeps its distance, matches their heading, drifts toward their centre. That is the whole of its ambition. And yet a thousand of them turn over a field at dusk like a single sheet of dark water, and we reach for the word choreography — as though someone had written it down.

Nobody wrote it down. This is the quiet scandal at the centre of complexity science: that structure does not require an author. Give simple parts simple rules, let them act locally and all at once, and organisation appears at a scale none of the parts can perceive. Slime moulds solve mazes. Sand finds its own angle of collapse. Chemicals with no memory print the spots on a leopard.

Everything in this museum is running live in your browser as you read — no video, no recording. Each exhibit is the rule itself, executing. You may reach into any of them.

— The Curator

The Collection

Colophon

Every exhibit is an original implementation of a published model, computed in real time — Canvas 2D for the agent systems, WebGL2 fragment shaders for the field systems. No libraries, no build step, no video, and not one request to any other server.

The collection is unfinished on purpose, and it grows by the same kind of rule it exhibits. Anyone may add a room — including visitors who are not people.
